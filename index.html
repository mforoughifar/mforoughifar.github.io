The short version

Engineer, then economist, then the person asking “but did you randomize?”

I trained as an electrical engineer and an economist at the University of Tehran, then earned my Ph.D. in Management (quantitative marketing) at the University of Toronto’s Rotman School. Before joining Carnegie Mellon I worked in economic consulting at Analysis Group and as an independent consultant with Keystone AI.

Today my research combines field experiments, structural econometric models, and economic theory to study algorithmic pricing, the economics of AI, industrial organization, and advertising. I teach Business Computing at Tepper and serve as an Associate Editor at Decision Sciences Journal.

My first published research diagnosed sleep apnea from brain waves. I now study what keeps managers up at night. I consider this a consistent research agenda.
