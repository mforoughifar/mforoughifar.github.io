Bring me a question. We’ll find out the answer properly.

For companies · field experiments

Your dashboard says the new feature “performed well.” Did it?

A field experiment is the difference between “metrics went up after launch” and “the launch caused it.” I design and analyze experiments with companies that want the second kind of answer, and I publish what we learn.

How a research partnership works

A 30-minute call

You describe the decision you’re facing. I tell you honestly whether an experiment can answer it.

A one-page design

What gets randomized, how many users or items we need, which metrics count, and how long it runs.

You run it, I watch it

Your team flips the switch. I monitor the data and handle the econometrics.

Answers, twice

A plain-English readout for your team, and a research paper for everyone else.

What you get

  • Causal answers. Not correlations, not “directionally positive.”
  • An independent view. I have no stake in the feature shipping.
  • Depth your team rarely has time for. Mechanisms, heterogeneity, long-run effects, and what to try next.
  • Credibility. Findings that hold up in front of a board, a regulator, or a peer reviewer.
  • Talent. Carnegie Mellon Ph.D. students who get to know your problem in depth.

What it looks like in practice

With a large sports media publisher, my coauthors and I randomized AI-generated summaries across 31,253 articles and tracked 8.1 million page views. The publisher learned exactly what summaries did to traffic, attention, and ad revenue, and where the losses came from. The publisher is not named in the paper.

Other field experiments include a learning platform tested across 63 classes in 12 schools, and a study of the header-bidding auctions publishers use to sell ads.

Questions companies usually ask

Will our name and data be public?

Only if you want them to be. Partners can remain anonymous in publications, and confidentiality agreements are a normal part of the process. We agree on what can be disclosed before any data changes hands.

What does it cost?

A research partnership is a trade rather than a purchase: you bring the question and the setting, I bring the design and analysis, and the results can be published. If you need work that will stay private, that’s a consulting engagement; see below.

We already run A/B tests. Why do we need you?

You may not. But standard A/B tests answer “which version wins this week?” The harder questions are about why, for whom, what happens in the long run, and what happens when competitors or users adapt. That is where economics earns its keep.

How long does it take?

It depends on your traffic and the size of the effect we’re looking for. Design usually takes a few weeks; the experiment runs as long as the statistics require. The paper takes longer. That part is my problem, not yours.

We don’t have a question yet, just a lot of data.

That’s a fine place to start. Tell me what decisions keep coming up, and I’ll tell you which of them your data can answer.
