Impact

Why this research matters.

Algorithms now set prices, rank products, summarize the news, and decide which ads people see. My research supplies the evidence that firms, regulators, and the public need to make those systems work well, and it speaks directly to priorities the United States has set for itself.

National importance

Five areas where my research addresses U.S. national priorities.

For each area: the national interest at stake, and what my work contributes to it.

1

American leadership in AI depends on adoption, not only invention.

The White House’s America’s AI Action Plan (July 2025) states that the bottleneck to realizing AI’s potential is “the limited and slow adoption of AI,” and names distrust and lack of understanding of the technology among the causes. Why people and firms distrust capable AI tools, and what makes them adopt, is the central question of my research program.

What my research contributes

  • Diagnosis. My Marketing Science paper shows that sellers on Airbnb rejected an AI pricing tool mainly because they underestimated its performance, and quantifies how much value is lost as a result.
  • Remedy. My work on communicating uncertainty shows, in randomized experiments, that AI tools which disclose their uncertainty are followed more closely.
  • Access. My article in Management and Business Review identifies the barriers that keep firms from the data, models, computing power, and talent AI requires, and ways to lower them.

Who benefits

U.S. firms deploying AI products; small businesses and independent sellers deciding whether to rely on them; and agencies working to accelerate responsible AI adoption across the economy.

2

Fair prices and competitive markets when algorithms set the prices.

Pricing algorithms now influence what Americans pay for housing, travel, and everyday goods, and U.S. enforcers are paying attention: the Federal Trade Commission has studied how personal data is used to set individualized prices, and the Department of Justice has brought an antitrust case over algorithmic pricing in rental housing. Sound policy requires evidence on how these tools behave in real markets.

What my research contributes

  • Empirical evidence on how a widely deployed pricing algorithm performs, who uses it, and how its design affects sellers and the platform.
  • Economic theory on how firms set prices when consumers learn about quality from imperfect information (International Journal of Research in Marketing).
  • Public explanation of algorithmic pricing for consumers through the press.

Who benefits

American consumers; small sellers who compete on platforms; and competition and consumer-protection authorities evaluating algorithmic pricing.

3

Housing and local economies: evidence for short-term rental policy.

Cities regulate short-term rentals to protect housing supply and neighborhoods, using tools that range from licensing to outright bans. Local governments usually have to choose among these tools with evidence from only one city at a time.

What my research contributes

  • One of the first large-scale, cross-city evaluations of short-term rental policies, covering 42 cities.
  • Estimates by policy type: bans, availability caps, licensing, and geographic limits have very different effects on listings and hosts.
  • Documentation of unintended consequences, such as lower service quality among remaining hosts.

Who benefits

City and state policymakers designing housing and tourism rules; residents and renters; and the hosts and platforms that must comply.

4

A trustworthy information ecosystem: news, ratings, and advertising.

Advertising revenue funds most online journalism, and online ratings guide a large share of consumer purchases. Both are being reshaped by AI. The FTC’s 2024 rule banning fake reviews and testimonials reflects how much the integrity of this information matters to U.S. consumers and honest businesses.

What my research contributes

  • A field experiment across 31,253 news articles measuring how AI summaries change publisher traffic, reader attention, and advertising revenue.
  • Research on distortion in online ratings (major revision at Information Systems Research).
  • A field experiment on the design of header-bidding advertising auctions that fund publishers.

Who benefits

News publishers deciding how to deploy AI; the advertising industry; consumers who rely on reviews; and regulators concerned with the sustainability of journalism and the accuracy of online information.

5

Education technology that works for every student.

Federal policy has made technology and AI in education a priority, including Executive Order 14277, Advancing Artificial Intelligence Education for American Youth (April 2025). Whether classroom software narrows or widens achievement gaps depends on how it is designed.

What my research contributes

  • A randomized field experiment in 63 classes showing that team-based design narrows within-class achievement gaps and raises skills for every type of student.
  • A design principle, shared feedback and team recognition, that any learning platform can apply.

Who benefits

Students and teachers; school systems choosing learning software; and the EdTech companies that build it.
